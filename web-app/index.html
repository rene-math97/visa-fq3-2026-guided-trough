The Setup

Four Things That Are True Going In

Every figure is sourced. Where sources disagree, both are shown. Figures that are my own arithmetic on sourced inputs are marked computed.

01

Management Told You This Quarter Would Be the Weak One

On the FQ2 call, CFO Christopher Suh guided FQ3 net revenue growth to low double digits and explicitly flagged it as "the lowest growth quarter of the year."[3] Adjusted EPS growth was guided to mid- to high single digits, against the +20% Visa just delivered in FQ2. The stated causes are mechanical rather than fundamental: higher client incentive growth on deal timing, lapping the FQ3 2025 incentive low point, and lower volatility against tough year-ago comps, partly offset by second-half pricing actions. Operating expense growth is guided to low teens on FIFA World Cup marketing.

02

The Last Quarter Was Excellent and the Stock Still Fell

FQ2 2026 net revenue was $11,230M, up 17% (16% constant-dollar); adjusted EPS $3.31, up 20%, beating the $3.09 to $3.10 consensus by 7.1%, the widest surprise in the eight-quarter run.[1][7] Value-added services grew 27% constant-dollar and now represent 30% of net revenue. Visa Direct did 3.7 billion transactions, up 23%. The company returned $9.2B in the quarter and authorised a new $20.0B buyback.[1] Shares fell 1.4% in premarket anyway, having run 8.4% in the week into the print.[8] Positioning, not results, drove the reaction.

03

One Metric Has Not Moved in a Year

Cross-border volume excluding intra-Europe, constant-dollar, is the cleanest read on Visa's highest-yielding revenue line. It printed +16%, +13%, +11%, +11%, +11%, +11% across the last six quarters.[1][9] Four consecutive quarters at exactly the same number is a plateau, not noise. The FQ2 call attributed a 2.5 point deceleration in CEMEA to the Middle East conflict. Against that, Visa's own World Cup data showed cross-border transactions in host cities up nearly 20% year over year during the June 11 to July 19 window — but that window straddles the FQ3 and FQ4 boundary, so only part lands tonight.[10]

04

The Multiple Is Not Demanding by Visa's Own History

This is the part the bear case usually skips. Visa trades at 31.6× trailing and 26.0× forward earnings.[4] Zacks computes the rolling forward multiple at 24.34× against Visa's own 5-year median of 25.82×, which puts the stock roughly a turn and a half below its own norm.[5] On street numbers it is 27.6× FY2026 and 24.4× FY2027 computed. Meanwhile ROE is 60.35% and ROIC 54.32%.[4] The debate is not whether Visa is expensive against itself. It is whether the growth algorithm still supports the premium against the market.

Six for six here, and eight for eight over a longer window. The relevant question is not whether Visa beats — it is what the beat is measured against. Consensus for tonight sits at $3.23 adjusted EPS on net revenue of $11.35B, up 11.6%, though other vendors carry $11.383B and $11.39B.[5][11][12] The year-ago FQ3 2025 base is $2.98 EPS on $10.2B.[9]

What the Guidance Range Actually Implies
FQ3 2025 adj. EPS × ( 1 + guided growth ) = implied FQ3 2026 range
$2.98 × ( 1 + 5% to 9% ) = $3.13 to $3.25
  • $2.98 — FQ3 2025 actual adjusted EPS[9]
  • 5% to 9% — company guide of "mid- to high single digit" diluted EPS growth[3]
  • $3.23 — street consensus, which sits in the upper half of that implied band computed

The street is already positioned near the top of management's own guidance range. That is the tension in tonight's print: the eight-quarter beat streak is real, but the bar it has to clear is no longer conservative. A $3.25 print would be a beat on paper and roughly a non-event against the guide.

The Long-Dated Debate

Disintermediation: Threat, Hedge, and Legislation

None of this moves tonight's number. All of it moves the multiple applied to tonight's number, which is why it belongs in a preview rather than a footnote.

Stablecoin Settlement
$7B
Annualised run-rate as of March 2026, up roughly 50% sequentially. 160+ stablecoin-linked card programmes live or in development, with ~200% year-over-year volume growth on them.[13][3] Visa is building the rails rather than defending against them.

Jul 16

GENIUS Act
Jan 2027
Signed July 2025, effective no later than 2027-01-18. Implementation rulemaking across OCC, FDIC, Treasury and FinCEN was still incomplete as of April 2026.[15]
Agentic Commerce
OpenAI
Partnership announced 2026-06-10 to enable Visa payments inside AI-agent commerce: tokenised credentials, real-time authorisation, user-defined spend limits. No timeline, metrics or revenue disclosed.[16]
The Legal and Legislative Docket
  • Interchange settlement — preliminary approval granted. On 2026-06-09 a federal judge granted preliminary approval to the revised ~$38B Visa and Mastercard swipe-fee settlement covering roughly 12 million US merchants.[17] Visa recorded litigation special items of $311M in FQ2 2026, $707M in FQ1 2026 and $899M in FQ4 2025.[1] The specific interchange reduction terms and the final approval hearing date could not be sourced and are not stated here.
  • Credit Card Competition Act — reintroduced and endorsed. Reintroduced 2026-01-13 by Senators Durbin and Marshall, with President Trump endorsing it the morning of reintroduction. It would require banks above $100B in assets to enable at least two unaffiliated networks. It mandates network competition, not fee caps. Sponsors have signalled they will seek to attach it to larger packages in 2026.[18] Current bill number and committee status could not be verified.
  • DOJ debit monopolisation suit — survived dismissal. Filed 2024-09-24; the court denied Visa's motion to dismiss on 2025-06-23, finding the government adequately alleged Sherman Act violations.[19] Current 2026 status and any trial date could not be sourced.
  • Account-to-account competition. Zelle has moved $1.2 trillion across 160 million accounts and is expanding beyond peer-to-peer into small business.[20] FedNow volumes have grown sharply, though the period covered by the published figures was not stated in the source and they are therefore treated as directional only.

What Would Make This Wrong

Risks in Both Directions

The plateau is a peak

Med-High

Four quarters at +11% cross-border could be a floor holding or a rollover in progress. If FQ3 prints +10% or below, the market will read a year of stability as the top of the cycle, and the premium multiple loses its justification.

Counter: the World Cup drove host-city cross-border transactions up ~20%, and part of that lands in this quarter.

Incentives eat the revenue line

Medium

Client incentives are contra-revenue, grew 14% last quarter, and were guided higher. A revenue miss caused entirely by incentive timing looks identical to a demand miss in the first headline, and the stock will trade on the headline.

What to check: volume growth alongside the revenue line. If volumes hold and revenue misses, it is incentives.

Entering at the high

Medium

At $362.53 against a 52-week high of $365.14, the setup mirrors April, when a 7.1% EPS beat produced a negative reaction after an 8.4% pre-print run. Good news may already be in the price.

Counter: the forward multiple is still at or below Visa's own 5-year median, so the premium is relative to the market, not to its own history.

Legislative tail risk

Low-Med

The CCCA has bipartisan sponsorship and a presidential endorsement. It mandates routing competition rather than price caps, but a credible path to passage would compress the multiple well before any revenue impact appears.

Status: reintroduced January 2026; sponsors are seeking a larger vehicle. Bill number and committee status could not be verified.

Upside: the trough is the trade

Low-Med

Cuts both ways. If management pre-marked the weakest quarter and Visa still beats convincingly, the FQ4 setup improves mechanically as incentive comps ease and World Cup spend lands more fully. A guided trough that does not materialise is a re-rating catalyst.

Evidence: FY2026 guidance was already raised once at FQ2, from high-single-digit to low-double-digit revenue growth.

Model risk in this page

Medium

Two of the three EPS presets are my estimates, and all three multiples are judgement. There is no sourced consensus for the single most important metric on the page, cross-border ex intra-Europe, so its thresholds are inference from six quarters of history.

Guardrail: every assumption is exposed as a slider or labelled as computed. Nothing is buried in code.
